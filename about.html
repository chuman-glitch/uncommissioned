Public art once happened everywhere. Now it mostly happens through permits.

uncommissioned is a site-responsive exhibition that treats the city as both canvas and contested ground. It works outside commercial galleries and institutional patronage, and asks what public art becomes when commissions, committees and approvals are taken away.

1The project takes its method from unsanctioned public interventions of the 1960s and 70s and from street practice today, and gives that method a curated, documented and citable form.
2The exhibition is opposed to monumental permanence. Works are temporary by design.

Each edition invites artists to make temporary works in public space. They go in without an unveiling and without announced locations. Audiences come across them in alleys, transit stations, vacant lots, caves and coastlines.

Each edition then takes on a new landscape. The first spread across cities on five continents. The second gathered onto one island. Each is recorded on this site as a catalogue: every work, its site, its materials and its documentation.

uncommissioned is a curatorial project of Nōvo Collective, an experimental platform for artist-led models of production, circulation and collaboration.
